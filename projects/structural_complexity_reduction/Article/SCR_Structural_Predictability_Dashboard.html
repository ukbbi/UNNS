01

What the investigation tests

The central proposition: a generative structural description can identify an admissible region of a large nominal state space and predict a measurable boundary between that region and the physically relevant configurations. Four connected questions, and three levels of achievement kept strictly apart.

The exclusion-gap equivalence is an exact criterion for physical sufficiency; it is not by itself an algorithm for predicting an unknown gap. The predictive achievement lies in the UNNS-motivated procedure: grammar frozen, quantitative prediction registered from smaller systems before the larger instance was evaluated, then independently tested and formally certified for the specified finite model.

03

The exclusion gap

For a finite model whose grammar excludes at least one configuration: S_T(W) ⊆ S_G ⇔ W < Δ¬G, where Δ¬G is the energy of the cheapest grammar-excluded configuration above the global ground state. Sufficiency for every window is decided by one number.

NORMALIZED GAP δL = Δ¬G / kBT

All four sizes lie below 1: exact preservation of the 1 kBT window fails at every size studied. Band: the interval frozen for the 4×4 value.

3×3 PRESERVATION AT W = 1 kBT

Preregistered test. The target holds 26 states; the grammar contains 10 and excludes 16 — exactly predicted by the first excluded level at 0.943 kBT.
Window chosen with the 1×1 and 2×2 gaps known; blindness was to the 3×3 low-energy outcome only.

Prediction, certification and the exact correction

A gap prediction was frozen from the 1×1–3×3 evidence, evaluated numerically, then fixed exactly by a Lean 4 proof that does not trust the numerical optimizer.

05

Four axes, reported separately

G constructive contraction · P physical preservation (false negatives = 0) · T independent transfer · C charged computational consequence. A favourable contraction cannot compensate for a failed sufficiency test, and the reverse.

06

Sensitivity of the gap to the Hamiltonian

Proposition (proved in the text, not in Lean): |Δ′ − Δ| ≤ 2ε, ε = max|H′ − H|. Applied to the frozen couplings it is mostly uninformative — and the manuscript says so.

WORST-CASE 2ε / Δ (BOUND INFORMATIVE ONLY BELOW 1)

Dimension scaled by (1+p). Rows ≥ 1 cannot exclude a gap change as large as the gap itself.

INPUTS AND PERSISTENCE

Width or height scaled by (1+p) is an exact rescaling by (1+p)², so the scale-separated residual is 0. No perturbed model was solved.

08

The next scientific test: cross-domain predictive transfer

Domain independence is the ambition of the UNNS methodology, not something a single spin-ice case establishes. A proposal — no result — of what a transfer test would need:

A prediction fulfilled in an unrelated domain would be the first evidence the relation is not specific to spin ice; a failure would locate the limit of the methodology and be recorded as the unfavourable result here was.
