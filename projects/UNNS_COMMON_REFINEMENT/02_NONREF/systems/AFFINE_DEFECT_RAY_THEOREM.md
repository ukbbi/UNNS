# Affine Persistent Defect-Ray Theorem

## Status

**Proved in this project.**

This theorem resolves the question left open by the exact affine elementwise controls:

\[
\operatorname{rank}H\ge2,\qquad \operatorname{ARD}(H)>0
\quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
H\setminus P(H)\text{ is unbounded}.
\]

The answer is **yes**, and the conclusion is stronger than unboundedness.

---

# 1. Domain and notation

Let \(H\) be a positive affine monoid: a finitely generated additive submonoid of a lattice,
with

\[
H\cap(-H)=\{0\}.
\]

Let

\[
L=\operatorname{gp}(H),\qquad
r=\operatorname{rank}L,
\]

and let \(\mathcal A(H)\) be the finite atom set. Define

\[
\operatorname{ARD}(H)=|\mathcal A(H)|-r.
\]

For \(x,y\in H\), write

\[
x\le_H y
\]

when \(y-x\in H\).

An element \(x\in H\) is **primal** if whenever

\[
x\le_H y+z,
\]

there are \(x_1,x_2\in H\) with

\[
x=x_1+x_2,\qquad x_1\le_H y,\qquad x_2\le_H z.
\]

Let

\[
P(H)=\{x\in H:x\text{ is primal}\}.
\]

The established project correspondence says that primality is exactly elementwise
\(2\times2\) route closure.

---

# 2. The theorem

## Theorem — Persistent defect ray

Let \(H\) be a positive affine monoid with

\[
r=\operatorname{rank}H\ge2.
\]

If

\[
\operatorname{ARD}(H)>0,
\]

then there exist

- an atom \(a\in\mathcal A(H)\), and
- a nonzero element \(\rho\in H\),

such that

\[
\boxed{
 a+n\rho\notin P(H)
 \qquad\text{for every }n\in\mathbb N_0.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{H\setminus P(H)\text{ contains an infinite affine ray}.}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{H\setminus P(H)\text{ is unbounded}.}
\]

This is stronger than the theorem candidate that motivated this step.

---

# 3. Lemma 1 — ARD forces a non-prime atom

Because \(H\) is positive affine, it is atomic and its atoms generate \(H\).

If every atom were prime, then cancellation through atomic factorizations would force unique
factorization.  The atom map

\[
\mathbb N_0^{|\mathcal A(H)|}\to H
\]

would then be an isomorphism, and hence

\[
|\mathcal A(H)|=\operatorname{rank}H.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{ARD}(H)>0
\]

forces at least one atom \(p\) to be non-prime.

For an atom, primality and primal divisibility collapse to primeness: if an atom splits in a
primal decomposition, one summand must be zero.  Hence \(p\) is also non-primal.

---

# 4. Lemma 2 — irredundant atom-to-atom failure witness

Let \(p\) be a non-prime atom.  Then there are \(y,z\in H\) such that

\[
p\le_H y+z,
\qquad
p\not\le_H y,
\qquad
p\not\le_H z.
\]

Factor \(y+z\) into atoms.  Among all submultisets of those atomic factors whose sum is divisible
by \(p\), choose one minimal under inclusion.  Call its sum \(S\).

No atom in that submultiset equals \(p\), because otherwise \(p\) would divide \(y\) or \(z\).
Hence the minimal submultiset has at least two atoms.

Write

\[
S=p+t.
\]

Choose one atom \(q\) from the minimal submultiset and let \(w\) be the sum of the remaining
atoms.  Then

\[
\boxed{p+t=q+w.}
\]

Minimality gives

\[
p\not\le_H w.
\]

Also

\[
q\not\le_H t.
\]

Indeed, if \(t=q+u\), then

\[
p+q+u=q+w
\]

and cancellation would give

\[
p+u=w,
\]

contradicting \(p\not\le_H w\).

Thus every non-prime atom yields an **irredundant atom-pair witness**

\[
\boxed{
 p+t=q+w,
 \qquad p\not\le_H w,
 \qquad q\not\le_H t,
}
\]

with distinct atoms \(p,q\).

The same equality witnesses non-primality from either atomic corner.

---

# 5. Lemma 3 — one orientation misses the cone

Let

\[
C=\mathbb R_{\ge0}H\subseteq L\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb R.
\]

Because \(H\) is positive, \(C\) is pointed.

For distinct atoms \(p,q\), it is impossible that both

\[
p-q\in C
\]

and

\[
q-p\in C,
\]

because then the pointed cone would contain the nonzero line generated by \(p-q\).

Therefore, after possibly swapping \(p\) and \(q\), we may write the irredundant witness as

\[
\boxed{a+u=b+v}
\]

with

\[
a\not\le_H v,
\qquad
b\not\le_H u,
\qquad
\boxed{a-b\notin C}.
\]

---

# 6. Lemma 4 — higher rank supplies a nonzero preserving direction

Since \(C\) is a pointed rational polyhedral cone and

\[
a-b\notin C,
\]

some facet inequality of \(C\) is violated by \(a-b\).

Thus there is a facet functional \(\lambda\) such that

\[
\lambda(C)\ge0,
\qquad
\lambda(a-b)<0.
\]

Let

\[
F=C\cap\ker\lambda
\]

be the corresponding facet.

Now the dimension assumption becomes decisive.  Since

\[
r\ge2,
\]

we have

\[
\dim F=r-1\ge1.
\]

Because \(C\) is generated by \(H\), the nonzero face \(F\) contains a nonzero monoid element.
Choose

\[
0\ne\rho\in H\cap F.
\]

Then

\[
\lambda(\rho)=0.
\]

Consequently, for every \(n\ge0\),

\[
\lambda(a-b+n\rho)
=
\lambda(a-b)<0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{a-b+n\rho\notin C}
\]

and therefore certainly

\[
\boxed{a-b+n\rho\notin H.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{b\not\le_H a+n\rho\quad\text{for every }n\ge0.}
\]

This is precisely the propagation mechanism that does not exist in rank one: a rank-one pointed
cone has no nonzero facet direction.

---

# 7. Proof of the theorem

For \(n\ge0\), define

\[
X_n=a+n\rho.
\]

Translate the irredundant witness along the facet direction:

\[
X_n+u
=
(a+n\rho)+u
=
b+(v+n\rho).
\]

Assume for contradiction that \(X_n\) is primal.

Because

\[
X_n\le_H b+(v+n\rho),
\]

primality gives

\[
X_n=x_1+x_2,
\qquad
x_1\le_H b,
\qquad
x_2\le_H v+n\rho.
\]

Since \(b\) is an atom, its only divisors are \(0\) and \(b\).  Therefore

\[
x_1=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
x_1=b.
\]

### Case 1: \(x_1=0\)

Then

\[
X_n\le_H v+n\rho.
\]

Cancelling the common translation gives

\[
v-a\in H,
\]

so

\[
a\le_H v,
\]

contradicting irredundancy.

### Case 2: \(x_1=b\)

Then

\[
b\le_H X_n,
\]

so

\[
a-b+n\rho\in H.
\]

But the facet functional gives

\[
\lambda(a-b+n\rho)<0,
\]

whereas every element of \(H\subseteq C\) has nonnegative \(\lambda\)-value.  Contradiction.

Both cases are impossible.  Hence

\[
X_n\notin P(H)
\]

for every \(n\ge0\).

Since \(\rho\ne0\) and \(H\) is torsion-free, the elements

\[
a,\ a+\rho,\ a+2\rho,\ldots
\]

are distinct and unbounded.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{H\setminus P(H)\text{ contains an infinite affine ray}.}
\]

QED.

---

# 8. Dimension-sensitive corollary

Combine this theorem with the established rank-one elementwise result.

For a nonfree positive affine monoid \(H\):

## Rank one

After normalization to a numerical monoid, the gap set is finite, so

\[
\boxed{H\setminus P(H)\text{ is finite}.}
\]

There is a cofinal all-primal tail.

## Rank at least two

The theorem above gives

\[
\boxed{H\setminus P(H)\text{ contains an infinite affine ray}.}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ccl}
\operatorname{rank}H=1 &:& \text{relation defect can be exhausted},\\[2mm]
\operatorname{rank}H\ge2 &:& \text{relation defect has a persistent direction}.
\end{array}
}
\]

This is an exact structural transition, not merely a feature of the retained examples.

---

# 9. Relation to ARD

The result can now be appended to the established affine criterion:

\[
\operatorname{ARD}(H)=0
\iff
H\cong\mathbb N_0^r
\iff
P(H)=H.
\]

For \(r\ge2\):

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ARD}(H)>0
\Longrightarrow
\exists a,\rho\ne0\ \forall n\ge0:\ a+n\rho\notin P(H).
}
\]

So in higher rank, a positive atom-relation defect does more than produce one failed square.  It
forces a **persistent defect channel**.

---

# 10. Conway / UNNS consequence

The finite/transfinite comparison is now substantially sharper.

Rank one allows

\[
\text{local obstruction}
\to
\text{finite gap core}
\to
\text{eventual primality}.
\]

Higher rank proves that this cannot be the general mechanism.  Once an independent atomic relation
exists, cone geometry supplies a direction along which a non-primal witness can be translated
forever:

\[
\text{atomic relation}
\to
\text{irredundant atom-pair witness}
\to
\text{violated facet}
\to
\text{persistent defect ray}.
\]

Therefore the omnific support-class descent should not be interpreted as merely moving beyond a
bounded bad region.  The stronger requirement is to prevent any defect channel from surviving under
descent and transport.

The UNNS-level statement supported by the mathematics is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{higher-dimensional route failure is directional, not merely local.}
}
\]

The successful Conway architecture must therefore establish primal traceability at the structural
base and preserve it through transport; scale alone cannot wash out a relation defect.

---

# 11. Scope

This theorem concerns **positive affine monoids** and uses:

1. finite atomic generation;
2. cancellativity and conicality;
3. the polyhedral cone \(C=\mathbb R_{\ge0}H\);
4. rank at least two;
5. \(\operatorname{ARD}(H)>0\).

It does not assert the same ray conclusion for arbitrary non-affine cancellative monoids.

The rank assumption is essential to this proof and to the rank-one contrast: in rank one a facet is
zero-dimensional, so there is no nonzero facet direction along which the obstruction can be
preserved.
