# Decisive Comparative Synthesis

## Question

Do positive integers, the successful affine controls, and the candidate omnific proof actually
share the same structural law — and is that law absent in the exact failure systems?

## Answer

\[
\boxed{\textbf{Yes.}}
\]

The shared law is not gcd structure, atomic freeness, or well-founded descent.

It is:

\[
\boxed{\textbf{global primality / factor traceability}.}
\]

For a factor \(a\), primality says:

\[
a\mid cd
\Longrightarrow
a=ef,\quad e\mid c,\quad f\mid d.
\]

In a cancellative commutative setting this is exactly the statement that every product equality
passing through \(a\) can be resolved into a four-factor common-refinement square.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every element primal}
\iff
\text{global four-factor refinement}.
}
\]

## Successful regimes

### Positive integers

The gcd/coprime construction proves traceability.

### Free rank-one and free affine controls

Prime atoms / independent coordinates prove traceability.

### Candidate omnific proof

Finite-class primality + local quotient refinement + strict support-class descent + transport/splice
prove traceability transfinally.

## Failure regimes

### \(H=\langle2,3\rangle\)

The atom \(2\) is not primal. The equality

\[
2+4=3+3
\]

is both:
- a non-refinement witness;
- a direct witness of failed factor traceability.

### Higher-rank affine relation systems

A nontrivial atomic relation produces a non-prime atom, hence failed traceability and failed global
refinement.

## The decisive separation

The **law** is:

\[
\text{traceability}\iff\text{refinement}.
\]

The **mechanisms** differ:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{domain} & \text{mechanism establishing traceability}\\
\hline
\mathbb N_{>0} & \gcd+\text{coprimality}\\
m\mathbb N_0,\ \mathbb N_0^r & \text{free coordinate routing}\\
\text{positive affine free monoids} & \text{prime atoms / atomic independence}\\
\mathbf{Oz}\text{ candidate} & \text{local quotient refinement + ordinal descent + transport}
\end{array}
\]

So the original refinement-plan question is now answered at the algebraic-structural level.
