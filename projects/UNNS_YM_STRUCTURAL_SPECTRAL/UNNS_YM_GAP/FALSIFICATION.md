# Falsification Criteria

## 1. Principle

The project is designed so that the central claim can fail.

A structural effect that survives only by changing definitions after seeing the results is not accepted.

## 2. Direct falsification of vacuum non-crossability

The strong non-crossability hypothesis is falsified for a tested observable family if an adversarial branch can be continued toward zero gap proxy while:

- remaining admissible in the frozen canonical chamber;
- preserving its structural regime under the predefined refinements;
- approaching the vacuum reference without a stable positive boundary.

## 3. Refinement falsification

A candidate boundary fails the refinement test if its positive separation systematically vanishes:

\[
b_r \rightarrow 0
\]

as lattice spacing/resolution is refined, within the validated range and uncertainty model.

A coarse-grid obstruction that disappears on finer data is classified as an artifact candidate.

## 4. Representation falsification

The strong cross-representation claim fails if two physically equivalent gauge-invariant representations give incompatible limiting conclusions and the discrepancy cannot be traced to a documented information loss or invalid transformation.

## 5. Null falsification

Specificity fails if matched null families reproduce the same boundary event, refinement persistence, and structural metrics at a comparable rate.

In that case the result may describe a generic property of ordered decays rather than Yang–Mills structure.

## 6. Physical-branch falsification

The proposed gapless-specific obstruction fails if the matched physical finite-gap branch loses admissibility under the same refinement at the same or greater rate.

This indicates pipeline fragility rather than a vacuum boundary.

## 7. Vacuum-reference falsification

A test is invalidated if the result changes qualitatively under equally justified vacuum baselines and no independent principle selects one baseline.

The experiment may be redesigned, but the original run is recorded as inconclusive.

## 8. Finite-volume falsification

A candidate positive boundary is not interpreted as a mass-gap signal if it scales consistently with a finite-size floor and disappears as volume increases.

## 9. Bridge falsification

Even a strong structural boundary does not support a physical mass-gap conclusion if no controlled relation can be established between the UNNS structural separation and a physical energy/correlation scale.

This outcome preserves the structural result while falsifying the stronger interpretation.

## 10. Continuum falsification

A Yang–Mills mass-gap interpretation fails if the structural boundary is tied to nonzero lattice spacing and vanishes under credible continuum extrapolation.

## 11. Overfitting indicators

The following invalidate a claimed preregistered result:

- modifying `STRUC-I` after viewing target labels;
- choosing normalization solely because it maximizes massive/gapless separation;
- discarding refinement levels solely because they weaken the desired result;
- selecting a null family after seeing which nulls fail to challenge the result;
- reporting only successful operator channels without the preregistered full set.

## 12. Outcome classes

### CONFIRMED STRUCTURAL BOUNDARY
All preregistered structural, refinement, and null criteria pass.

### PARTIAL
A structural boundary appears but cross-representation, refinement, or specificity requirements are incomplete.

### ARTIFACT-CANDIDATE
The boundary weakens systematically under refinement, volume increase, or representation change.

### NO SEPARATION
Massive and gapless families remain structurally indistinguishable under the frozen protocol.

### INCONCLUSIVE
Data quality, vacuum reference, or refinement coverage prevents a valid test.

## 13. Strongest allowed conclusion by evidence

- Level A: “A structural separation is observed.”
- Level B: “A refinement-stable structural boundary is observed.”
- Level C: “A cross-representation refinement-stable vacuum-boundary obstruction is observed.”
- Level D: “The structural boundary is quantitatively linked to a physical gap proxy.”
- Level E: only after a mathematically controlled bridge and continuum argument may the project discuss implication of a positive spectral lower bound.

No lower evidence level is relabeled as a proof of the Yang–Mills mass gap.
