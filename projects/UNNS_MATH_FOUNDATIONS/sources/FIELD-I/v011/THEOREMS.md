# FIELD-I v011 — THEOREMS (T64–T67): Prime-Survivor Selection and Static Residue Certification

Status tags: **PROVED** (hand proof from earlier theorems; tests are consistency checks, not the proof), **DATA**, **OPEN**.
Notation as in v009/v010: persistent orbit with G = g, x = γL²/g, exponent state E = (e_p), o_p = ord_g(p), w = γ+1; T49–T63 unchanged.
A *reset state* is a state with γ = 1. For M ≥ 2 and a state E, M = ∏p^{j_p} is **available** if there is a ∈ ∏_{q∤M}{q^i : 0 ≤ i ≤ e_q} with ∏_{p|M}p^{e_p+j_p}·a ≡ 1 (mod g).

Question of v011 (O10.1): decide, from finite arithmetic data, **which prime carries forever**.

## T67 — The exact carry-level predictor (PROVED; T53 + T54 as a formula; used by T64–T66)

**Statement.** From a reset state E (exact G = g) of a persistent orbit, the next carry is
 **M* = min{ M ≥ 2 : M available at E }**,
the next state is E' = E with e_p increased by v_p(M*) for every p | M*, γ' = 1 again (a reset state), and the carry occupies the γ-steps 1, 2, …, M*²−1, i.e. w = 2, …, M*².
The orbit remains persistent through this carry iff M*² < g (g odd prime; in general iff additionally gcd(g,w) = 1 for all w ≤ M*², e.g. when every prime factor of g exceeds M*²).
**Consequently every state after the first reset state is a reset state**, and the whole orbit is the iteration E ↦ E' of this map; there is no partial absorption after the first reset.
*Proof.* By T53 a divisor d ≡ 1 (mod g) is available at the step w iff M(d)² | w, M(d) = the new part. Write d = ∏_{p|M}p^{e_p+j_p}·a with M = M(d) and a built from the primes q ∤ M with v_q(a) ≤ e_q:
this is exactly "M is available". The first w ≥ 2 for which some d has M(d) > 1 and M(d)² | w is w = M*², M* = min M(d) (T54: from γ = 1 the plateau runs to w = M*²). At w = M*² the available new parts are those M(d) with M(d) | M*; by minimality only M(d) = M*,
so r = lcm = M* and γ' = w/r² = 1. All w ≤ M*² are < g (g prime) hence h = 1 and persistence is automatic (T49). QED
*Test M1:* next_M reproduces the residue machine at every reset-to-reset carry of every reset seed tested, including composite carries (several primes at once, which occur in transients).

## T64 — The minimax selection theorem (PROVED): the survivor is argmin of the worst-case carry, independently of the phases

**Setting (fixed independent odometers).** Primes p_1 < … < p_k, each with a nonempty set Abs_i ⊂ Z/o_i, state e_i ∈ Z/o_i; the pending carry is c_i(e_i) = p_i^{gap_i(e_i)}, gap_i(e) = min{j ≥ 1 : (e+j) mod o_i ∈ Abs_i};
the smallest pending carry fires and e_i ← e_i + gap_i(e_i). Put d_i = the largest cyclic gap of Abs_i (the largest difference of consecutive elements, taking o_i + a_1 − a_r for the wrap-around) and
 **m_i = p_i^{d_i}** (the worst-case carry).
**Theorem.** (i) c_i(e) ≤ m_i for every phase e. (ii) Every walk, from every start phase, ends in a cycle in which exactly one prime fires, namely **p_* = argmin_i m_i**. (iii) With a window (a pending carry ≥ W is forbidden, i.e. the orbit leaves persistence), the system stays admissible forever **iff m_{p_*} < W**; if m_{p_*} ≥ W every walk exits in finite time.
*Proof.* (i) e lies in a cyclic interval between consecutive elements of Abs_i (or is one of them); gap(e) is the distance to the right end, hence ≤ the length of that interval ≤ d_i. (ii) Some prime fires infinitely often (finitely many primes, each step fires one). Suppose r fires infinitely often and r ≠ p_*.
Then r's phase takes every value of Abs_r cyclically, in particular the one before a gap of length d_r, where r's pending carry is m_r. There r fires only if m_r < c_{p_*}(e_{p_*}) ≤ m_{p_*} by (i). But m_r > m_{p_*} (m's of distinct primes are distinct, and p_* has the smallest). Contradiction, so only p_* fires infinitely often.
(iii) If m_{p_*} < W, the smallest pending carry is ≤ c_{p_*} ≤ m_{p_*} < W at every step, so no exit. If m_{p_*} ≥ W, p_* eventually sits in its worst-case phase where the smallest pending carry is m_{p_*} ≥ W (every competitor's pending carry is frozen above it): exit. QED
This sharpens T60 (which proved only "at most one") to a **non-iterative, phase-independent selection rule**. It does not by itself apply to the collective map: there the odometer tables are not fixed (see T66).
*Tests B1 (exhaustive, three primes, all subsets of Z/s for s ≤ 4, all phases), B2 (random, 2–5 primes), B3 (window criterion).*

## T65 — The quotient-residue formula (PROVED)

Let p be a prime with gcd(p,g) = 1, H ⊂ (Z/g)* the subgroup generated by the residues of the **saturated** other primes (exponent ≥ ord−1), F the set of residues of the divisors of the frozen (unsaturated or inert) other primes' part, and t_p the order of pH in (Z/g)*/H. Put
 S_p = { r mod t_p : p^r·f ∈ H for some f ∈ F }.
Then **Abs_p = { k mod o_p : k mod t_p ∈ S_p }** (a union of residue classes mod t_p, t_p | o_p) and the gaps of Abs_p in Z/o_p are the gaps of S_p in Z/t_p, so
 **m_p = p^{maxgap(S_p)}** (maxgap taken cyclically in Z/t_p).
For prime g, t_p = o_p / gcd(o_p, |H|).
*Proof.* Abs_p = {k : p^{−k} ∈ 𝒜_{−p}} with 𝒜_{−p} = H·F (the saturated primes contribute whole cyclic subgroups, whose product is H). p^{−k} ∈ H·F iff p^{−k}H = fH for some f, i.e. iff p^{k}f ∈ H for some f ∈ F... equivalently p^{k}H = f^{−1}H.
This depends only on k mod t_p since p^{t_p} ∈ H. For prime g the unit group is cyclic, |⟨p⟩ ∩ H| = gcd(o_p, |H|), so t_p = o_p/|⟨p⟩∩H|. QED
This replaces "walk the odometer" by a finite computation in a quotient group; it also works for composite g (the quotient is then not cyclic, t_p is the plain order). It generalises T62 (two saturated clocks) to any saturated background.
*Tests Q1 (identity with the definition on random cases including composite and even g), Q2 (the formula for t_p).*

## T66 — Static survivor certificate (PROVED; sufficient, and necessary for saturated survivors)

**Hypotheses (finite data of one reset state E).** g prime, or more generally g with no prime factor ≤ m² (S5). G(x) = g exactly; E is a reset state (γ = 1). A prime r ∤ g is *saturated* in E: e_r ≥ ord_g(r) − 1. Let Abs_r be computed from the other primes' residue sets (T56/T65),
d_r its largest cyclic gap, m = r^{d_r}.
 (S3) m² < g and (S5) no prime factor of g is ≤ m².
 (S4) **no M with 2 ≤ M < m and gcd(M, r) = 1 is available at E.**
**Theorem.** Under S3–S5, the orbit from E is persistent forever and **only r carries**: the carries are r^{j}, j running cyclically through the gaps of Abs_r (starting at the phase e_r mod o_r); the γ-period is Σ(r^{2j_i}−1) over one period s of Abs_r,
ρ = r^s, x_{n+P} = r^{2s}x_n for all n ≥ 0 (not only eventually). Conversely, for prime g: if r is saturated, E is a reset state, and the orbit carries only r from E on and stays persistent, then S3 and S4 hold (S5 is automatic for prime g > m²).
*Proof.* **Sufficiency.** Induction on carries. Let E be a reset state with S3–S5 and r saturated. Let M* be the smallest available M (T67).
 (a) M* has no prime factor outside r unless it is coprime to r: if M = r^a·s with a ≥ 1, s > 1, s coprime to r is available whenever M is, because r is saturated: choose i ∈ [0, o_r−1] with i ≡ e_r + a (mod o_r); then i ≤ e_r, and d' = d·r^{i−e_r−a} is a smaller divisor ≡ d ≡ 1 with new part s (the T55 argument).
 So the smallest available M is either a pure power r^a or coprime to r. (b) A coprime M is not smaller than m by S4... precisely: S4 excludes all coprime M < m, and a coprime M equal to m is impossible (m is a power of r), so every coprime available M exceeds m.
 (c) A pure power r^a is available iff (e_r + a) mod o_r ∈ Abs_r (T56); the smallest such a is the gap at the current phase, so r^{a} ≤ m. Hence M* = r^{gap(φ)} ≤ m < every coprime available M, and M*² ≤ m² < g, with gcd(g,w) = 1 for all w ≤ M*² by S5. By T67 the carry is r^{gap}, the state after it is E' = E with e_r += gap, again a reset state, r still saturated,
 all other exponents unchanged. Abs_r and the availability of coprime M do not change: the residue set of r in "a" is the full subgroup ⟨r⟩ (saturated), and every coprime M ignores r's exponent. So S3–S5 hold at E', and the induction continues; the phase moves along the gaps of Abs_r.
 **Necessity.** If r carries forever, it visits the phase at which its pending carry is m (T64(i) equality: the phase before the largest gap), where M* = m; any available coprime M would have to be > m (it cannot equal a power of r), hence none is < m (S4); persistence of the orbit gives m² < g (T52, the carry m passes through w = g otherwise). For prime g, S5 is then automatic. QED
**Unsaturated extension T66′.** If e_r < ord_g(r) − 1, apply T67 to the phases e_r, e_r + gap, … until e_r ≥ ord_g(r) − 1 (at most ord_g(r) − 1 carries; only r's exponent moves as long as each next_M equals the predicted r-power), then T66. This is a finite check over r-carries only (no γ-level orbit).
**Uniqueness.** At most one prime is certified from a state: if r_1, r_2 were both certified, S4 for r_1 gives c_{r_2} > m_{r_1} ≥ c_{r_1} and S4 for r_2 gives c_{r_1} > m_{r_2} ≥ c_{r_2}: c_{r_2} > c_{r_1} > c_{r_2}. (Test S1, S3.)
**What is NOT certified.** The competitor condition is about the *actual* state; the hypothetical tables of T64 for other primes are not used (a competitor's Abs depends on r being saturated). Seeds that are not reset states (γ₀ > 1) and primes with composite/even g with small factors are outside the theorem (OPEN).
*Tests S0–S5: soundness against the γ-level residue machine (the machine does not use next_M), completeness on exact eventual cycles, uniqueness, negatives, coverage; C1: the g = 31 example L₀ = 2⁵·101² with the exact direct orbit of C (resets at 15, 30, 33; x_{n+33} = 2¹⁰x_n).*

## T68 — Consequences (PROVED)

(a) **Every persistent orbit reaches a statically certified reset state** (after its eventual regime is entered, T51/T60; every eventual reset state satisfies S3–S5 for the active prime when g is prime, by the necessity part of T66). So "which prime survives" is decided by a finite residue test at that state.
(b) From a certified state the complete future is a function of finite data: carry word (gaps of Abs_r from the current phase), γ-period Σ(r^{2j}−1), ρ = r^s, window margin m² < g.
(c) Because the survivor's saturation matters, **g alone does not determine the survivor** (v009 already has g = 31 with survivor 2 and with survivor 5); the pair (g, background E) does.
