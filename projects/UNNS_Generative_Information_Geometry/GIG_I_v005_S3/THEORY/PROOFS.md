**LT-0.** Faces of P_k = ordered set partitions; the boundary of f=(B₁|…|B_s) is the sum of the refinements obtained by splitting one block B_j into an ordered pair (S|T) of non-empty parts. Fix f and a block B_i. The terms of ∂Ins_i(f) are: (α) splits of a block B_j, j≠i (rank 6 stays in the block i): these are Ins_i(g) for g ∈ ∂f splitting B_j; (β) splits of B_i∪{6} into (S|T) with 6 ∈ T, S≠∅: if T={6} the face ins_after(i) = f with {6} placed right after block i; otherwise (S | (B_i∖S)∪6) = Ins into the second part of the split g=(S|B_i∖S) of f; (γ) splits with 6 ∈ S: if S={6}, ins_before(i); otherwise Ins into the first part. Summing over i, every pair (g, block j of g) with g ∈ ∂f occurs exactly once: if j is one of the two parts created by splitting B_i it comes from (β)/(γ) of Ins_i, otherwise from (α) of Ins_l with l the original index of j. So Σ_i ∂Ins_i(f) = Σ_{g∈∂f} Σ_j Ins_j(g) + Σ_i(ins_before(i)+ins_after(i)). ins_after(i)=ins_before(i+1) (rank 6 between blocks i and i+1), so mod 2 only ins_before(1)=first(f) and ins_after(s)=last(f) survive. The singleton block {6} of first(g) cannot be split, so ∂first(g)=first(∂g); likewise last. (Verified for all faces of P₆ in tests.)
