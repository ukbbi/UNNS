Generative Information Geometry in the UNNS Substrate

UNNS admits general generative grammars; the monograph develops the theory for them and works the arithmetic case in full, the SELECT-II operator, which the integer explorer below demonstrates. A generated number can be reached by many routes. This monograph studies how many, how those routes are joined to one another, which arithmetic decides whether they leave a hole, and what an observer can recover from counts alone.

02Explore a generated number

The operator is TN(m) = m(N+1)²/N, defined when N divides m. Reading it backwards, a number t has a predecessor for every d ≥ 2 with d² | t, namely t(d−1)/d². A history of t is a maximal chain of predecessors; A(t) = log₂|Ft| is its ambiguity. Everything below is computed exactly in your browser.

t ↦ { t(d−1)/d² : d² | t, d ≥ 2 }

Anchored state (the two anchor families only) takes three values: absent (the source does not contain the anchor class), present but filled, and surviving. The exact radius-two identity
|B₂(w)| = 1 + d + s − q

For a vertex w of the swap graph: d its degree, s the number of two-step paths, q the number of squares through w. The identity contains no hexagon term, yet it does not by itself settle what radius-two data can see: the witnesses W5 and W6 do. Systems with identical radius-two accessibility signatures can have different hexagonal 2-cell counts and different first homology (, ).

07Forbidden histories carry the topology

A hole in the admissible histories is a cycle among the dead prefix sets. The chain of equivalences is exact for prefix-defined systems.

β₂(KA) = dim H₁(YA; 𝔽₂) = dim H₁(N[DA])

Scope: six events (k = 6), with ∅ ≠ A ⊊ S₆ for the duality, and A prefix-defined for the nerve identification. For general k the shifted statement is H̃j(KA; 𝔽₂) ≅ H̃k−3−j(YA; 𝔽₂), homology on the left and cohomology on the right.

08Locality, not divisibility, is the dividing line

For six events, constraints on consecutive pairs never create a two-dimensional hole; constraints on consecutive triples can create five independent ones.

Pairwise grammars, k = 6 ()
β₂(KAG; 𝔽₂) = 0 for every digraph G, ∅ ≠ AG ⊊ S₆

Includes every symbolic grammar over a finite alphabet whose admissibility is a conjunction of consecutive-pair constraints, with arbitrary category multisets. The proof reduces to intersection checks over 30 single pieces, 435 pairs and 4,060 triples, all of which pass.

Triple constraints break it ()
A consecutive-triple system with N = 674 histories, f = (674, 1606, 1240, 320, 16), Betti numbers β = (1, 2, 5, 0, 0). Prefix-set constraints can also produce holes ().

12What is, and is not, claimed

  • The general claims are the chain-level calculus, the prefix law and its consequences, the reconstruction and duality theorems, and the radius-two identity. Everything naming a content, a source or a census is a finite computation with its range stated.
  • Census observations are not theorems and carry no claim beyond the census; their arithmetic origin is unexplained.
  • The pairwise locality theorem is for six labels; other sizes are not covered, and the triple version is false at six.
  • The survival law is proved for every cycle through its minimal filling families; it is an effective criterion for the anchors, not an algorithm in general.
  • The link to cellular resolutions of monomial modules is an interpretation resting on a cited theorem; the module and its resolution have not been computed.
  • Results are stated for the SELECT-II grammar and the prefix-defined and symbolic grammars treated; nothing is claimed for other UNNS operators.
  • An external line-by-line proof review of the monograph has not yet been carried out.
