## Theorem A5-T5.1 (towers are height filtrations of one complex) — PROVED
`D(x_k)={d | S_k : d≥2, d≡1 (m)}` and `S_k | S_{k′}` for k≤k′. The set `D_k` is `D_{k′}` cut by a **box-convex** height condition (valuation of the growing prime ≤ h): for u,v∈D_k every w in the divisor interval has height ≤ h. By the interval rule, edges among D_k are the same in K(D_k) and K(D_{k′}); likewise cells (all 1-faces edges). Hence **K_k is the induced subcomplex of K_{k′} on height ≤ h; K_k⊂K_{k+1}; every tower transition has Dead=∅** (the new occurrences sit in the top slab and cannot lie in an old interval). FINITE-EXACT audit: Z7 (both towers).
Consequence (with A5-T4.2/4.3): in a tower a Betti number can only be lowered by a new (i+1)-cell (killing classes); creation is relative homology of the new slab.

## Lemma A5-T5.2 (moves stabilise) — PROVED
Let `Λ=ker(Z^r→(Z/m)^*)` for the primes of S. A Graver vector `g` corresponds to a *minimal zero-sum sequence* over `⟨p_i⟩⊂(Z/m)^*` (g_i⁺ copies of p_i, g_i⁻ copies of p_i⁻¹); a zero-sum-free-subsequence argument on partial sums gives length `‖g‖₁ ≤ |⟨p_i⟩| ≤ φ(m)`. So all moves have ℓ₁ ≤ 10 (m=11) resp. 4 (m=5); once the cap of the growing prime exceeds this bound the move set is constant. (Z7: brute-force Graver of Λ, max ℓ₁ = 10 and 4 exactly.) The G=5 value 4 is consistent with β₁ being constant from k=3 (cap k+1≥4).

## Theorem A5-T5.3 (eventual quasi-polynomial Betti laws) — PROVED-SCOPED (qualitative)
Let the growing prime be `ρ` with order `ω=ord_m(ρ)` (ω=5 for ρ=3, m=11; ω=4 for ρ=2, m=5). For every field F and every i there are k₁ and a quasi-polynomial `q_i(k)` of **degree ≤ 1 and period dividing ω** with `β_i(K_k;F)=q_i(k)` for all `k≥k₁`.
*Proof sketch (complete in structure; the threshold k₁ is not controlled).* Let `K̃` be the complex of the full lattice slab `Λ∩(box × ℕ)` including the origin; the shift `T` by ω in the growing coordinate is a cellular injection (ρ^ω≡1). Every cell of K̃ is `T^n c₀` with c₀ in a finite set (cells have bounded diameter by A5-T5.2). K_k is the sub-complex of cells **avoiding the origin** with height ≤ 3+k. Form the Rees-type module `R=⊕_h C_*(K̃^{≤h})` over `F[t,T]` (t = inclusion, deg 1; T = shift, deg ω); R is generated by the finitely many `c₀`, and so is the sub-module R′ of origin-avoiding cells (the non-T-divisible cells contain T·0 or have min-height ≤ ω). By Noetherianity `H_i(R′)=⊕_h H_i(K_h)` is a finitely generated graded `F[t,T]`-module; its Hilbert function is eventually a quasi-polynomial of degree ≤ dim−1 = 1 and period | lcm(1,ω) (Hilbert–Serre, weighted). ∎

## Comparison of the two towers (what predicts what)
* Numerical multiplier/period are sieve data (FIRST_EVENT.md) and say nothing by themselves about topology: both towers have Dead=∅, but different Betti vectors.
* The stabilisation scale is set by the Graver ℓ₁ bound `|⟨p_i⟩|` (4 vs 10) and by the order ω of the growing prime (4 vs 5): they control *when* a periodic regime can start and its *period* (Lemma A5-T5.2, Theorem A5-T5.3). The specific Betti values are properties of the cross-section (caps and Λ); **no theorem here explains why** G=11 has unbounded β₃ and G=5 has none — that is the open structural question (OPEN_PROBLEMS O5-3).
