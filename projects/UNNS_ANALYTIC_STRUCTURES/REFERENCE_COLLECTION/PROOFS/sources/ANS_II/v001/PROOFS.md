# ANS-II v001 — Analytic invariance and expansive torsion: proofs

Research notes. Uses ANS-I v001–v003 and the definitions of *Algebraic and Topological Extensions* v1.1 [AT] and *Torsion-Class Carry Dynamics* v1.0 [TC].

## 0. Setting
**Extension.** $A=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_m)$ is a finite list of multipliers $\alpha_j>1$ with a rational power, $\alpha_j=\prod_pp^{w_{jp}}$, $w_{jp}\in\mathbb Q$. Letters are the nesting labels $N\ge1$ (multiplier $r_N$) and the symbols $\alpha_j$. Write $\mu(\ell)$ for the multiplier of a letter and $X_p(\ell)$ for its exponent at $p$: $X_p(N)=2v_p(N+1)-v_p(N)$, $X_p(\alpha_j)=w_{jp}$. Let $P_0$ be the finite set of primes with some $w_{jp}\ne0$, and $R=\prod_{p\in P_0}p$.

**States and admissibility [TC].** States are $x=a_\varphi n$, $n\in\mathbb N$, where $a_\varphi=\prod p^{\varphi_p}$, $0\le\varphi_p<1$, and $\varphi$ runs over the finite group $\Phi$ generated by the vectors $w_j$ modulo $\mathbb Z$. A letter is admissible at $x$ when $\mu(\ell)x$ again has all prime exponents non-negative (integrality preservation). For a nesting letter this is $N\mid n$.

**Histories.** For a word $w$ and a class $\varphi$, $K_\varphi(w)$ is the least $n$ such that $w$ is admissible from $a_\varphi n$; $Q(w)=\prod\mu$; $E_\varphi(w)=a_\varphi K_\varphi(w)Q(w)$. Put $\mathcal G_\varphi(s,t)=\sum_wt^{|w|}E_\varphi(w)^{-s}$, $A_\varphi(X)=\#\{w:E_\varphi(w)\le X\}$, and let $\mathcal Z_\varphi(s,t)$ be the sum of $t^{|w|}(\text{endpoint})^{-s}$ over all pairs (source $a_\varphi n$, admissible $w$).

**Characteristic function.** For $a>1$, $B_A(a)=\sum_N r_N^{-a}+\sum_j\alpha_j^{-a}$. It decreases from $\infty$ to $0$; $\sigma_A$ is the root of $B_A=1$. The letter law is $\nu_a(\ell)=\mu(\ell)^{-a}/B_A(a)$ and $\mu_p(a)=\mathbb E_{\nu_a}X_p$ is the **drift** at $p$.

**Hypothesis (D).** $\mu_p(\sigma_A)>0$ for every $p\in P_0$.

## 1. Classwise invariance (ANS2-T00)
**Theorem.** Take $A$ empty and any base point $\alpha_C>0$, with admissibility $N\mid n$ on $\alpha_Cn$ ([AT], classwise decomposition). Then $\mathcal Z_C(s,t)=\alpha_C^{-s}\mathcal Z_H(s,t)$ and $N_{\alpha_Cx}(\alpha_CX)=N_x(X)$.

*Proof.* $w$ is admissible from $\alpha_Cn$ exactly when it is admissible from $n$, and its endpoint is multiplied by $\alpha_C$. $\square$

Hence the abscissa, the suppression bounds and the derivative bounds of ANS-I hold in every such class (the factor $\alpha_C^{-s}$ is entire and has no zeros).

## 2. Source formula and comparison of classes
**Lemma 1 (ANS2-L1).** With $S_p(j)$ the sum of $X_p$ over the first $j$ letters, $v_p(K_\varphi(w))=\max_{0\le j\le|w|}\big(-\lfloor\varphi_p+S_p(j)\rfloor\big)$, and $w$ is admissible from $a_\varphi n$ exactly when $K_\varphi(w)\mid n$.

*Proof.* The state after $j$ letters has exponent $\varphi_p+v_p(n)+S_p(j)$ at $p$. Admissibility means that all these are non-negative, that is $v_p(n)\ge-\lfloor\varphi_p+S_p(j)\rfloor$ for all $j$, because $v_p(n)$ is an integer. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (ANS2-L2).** $K_\varphi(w)\mid K_0(w)\mid R\,K_\varphi(w)$. Hence for real $a>0$ and each depth $k$,
$$a_\varphi^{-a}\,\mathcal G_{0,k}(a)\le\mathcal G_{\varphi,k}(a)\le(R/a_\varphi)^{a}\,\mathcal G_{0,k}(a).$$

*Proof.* $\lfloor S\rfloor\le\lfloor\varphi_p+S\rfloor\le\lfloor S\rfloor+1$ for $p\in P_0$, with $\varphi_p=0$ otherwise. Then $a_\varphi E_0/R\le E_\varphi\le a_\varphi E_0$. $\square$

So all classes have the same abscissa and the same depth exponents. No hypothesis on signs or drift is used.

**Factorization (ANS2-T01).** $\mathcal Z_\varphi(s,t)=\zeta(s)\,\mathcal G_\varphi(s,t)$ where convergent: the admissible sources are $n=K_\varphi(w)m$, with endpoint $E_\varphi(w)m$.

**Growth along a word.** $K_\varphi(w)\mid K_\varphi(w\ell)$, so $E_\varphi(w\ell)\ge\mu(\ell)E_\varphi(w)\ge\lambda E_\varphi(w)$ with $\lambda=\min(4,\min_j\alpha_j)>1$. In particular only finitely many words have $E_\varphi\le X$.

**Representation.** Under independent letters of law $\nu_a$,
$$\mathcal G_{\varphi,k}(a)=B_A(a)^k\,a_\varphi^{-a}\,\mathbb E\,K_{\varphi,k}^{-a}.$$

## 3. Positive drift
**Lemma 3.** Under (D) there are a neighbourhood $I$ of $\sigma_A$, $\eta>0$ and $M_0$ such that $\mathbb E\,e^{-\eta X_p}\le1$ for all primes $p$, all $a\in I$, and letters restricted to $N\le M$ (all $\alpha_j$ kept), $M_0\le M\le\infty$. If all $w_{jp}\ge0$, then (D) holds and every compact interval $I\subset(1,\infty)$ may be used.

*Proof.* For $p\notin P_0$ the letters $\alpha_j$ have $X_p=0$, and ANS-I v002 Lemma B treats the nesting letters ($p\le M$; for $p>M$ one has $X_p\ge0$). For the finitely many $p\in P_0$, $f(\eta)=\mathbb Ee^{-\eta X_p}$ is finite for small $|\eta|$, $f(0)=1$, $f'(0)=-\mu_p(a)<0$; choose $\eta$ by continuity in $a$, then $M_0$ by dominated convergence. If $w_{jp}\ge0$, the nesting part of the drift is already positive (the injection of ANS-I v002 Lemma B gives $\mathbb E\,v_p(N)\le\mathbb E\,v_p(N+1)$) and the other part is non-negative. $\square$

By Ville's inequality and Lemma 1, $\Pr(v_p(K_{\varphi,k})\ge r)\le e^{-\eta(r-1)}$ for $r\ge1$: such an event forces $S_p(j)\le-(r-1)$ for some $j$. Hence $\mathbb E\,v_p(K_{\varphi,k})\le C$ uniformly in $p,k,\varphi,M$.

## 4. Depth bounds and abscissa (ANS2-T02)
**Theorem.** Under (D), for $a\in I$, $\beta=1/a$, $b>0$ and large $k$,
$$\frac{c\,k^{\beta}}{\log k}\le-\log\mathbb E\,K_{\varphi,k}^{-b}\le C\,k^{\beta}.$$

*Proof.* **Left inequality.** This is the argument of ANS-I v002 §4, applied to primes $q\notin P_0$. A nesting letter is *fresh* if some such $q$ occurs in $N(N+1)$ for the first time among nesting letters, and *successful* if such a $q$ divides $N$. The comparison $\nu_a(U(S))\le2^a\nu_a(D(S))$ is unaffected by the new normalisation, so a fresh letter succeeds with conditional probability at least $c_0=1/(1+2^a)$. No $\alpha_j$ changes the coordinate $q$, so at a success $S_q$ drops from $0$ below $0$ and $q\mid K_{\varphi,k}$; hence $K_{\varphi,k}\ge2^{Z_k}$ with $Z_k$ the number of successes. The supermartingale is unchanged. The primes of the window $(P,2P]$, $P=(k/\Lambda)^{1/a}$, $\Lambda=2\cdot8^aB_A(a)$, lie outside $P_0$ for large $k$, each occurs as a letter with probability at least $(8P)^{-a}/B_A(a)$, and the estimate for letters above $P^2$ is unchanged. So at least $m/20$ fresh letters occur outside an event of probability $e^{-cm}$, $m$ the number of primes in the window.

**Right inequality.** By Jensen it suffices that $\mathbb E\log K_{\varphi,k}=O(k^\beta)$. The primes of $P_0$ contribute $O(1)$ by §3. For $p\notin P_0$ let $\tau$ be the first step with a nesting letter divisible by $p$. Before $\tau$, $S_p\ge0$; after it, $-S_p(j)\le v_p(N_\tau)+\sup_{h\ge0}\big(S_p(\tau)-S_p(\tau+h)\big)$. Both terms have bounded conditional mean, so $\mathbb E\,v_p(K_{\varphi,k})\le C\Pr(\tau\le k)\le C'\min(1,kp^{-a})$, and $\sum_p\log p\,\min(1,kp^{-a})=O(k^\beta)$. $\square$

**Corollaries** (under (D)). (i) $\mathcal G_{\varphi,k}(a)^{1/k}\to B_A(a)$, and the abscissa of $\mathcal G_\varphi(\cdot,1)$ is exactly $\sigma_A$; for divergence below $\sigma_A$ use $a\in I$ and monotonicity ($E_\varphi\ge1$). The counting statement $A_\varphi(X)=X^{\sigma_A+o(1)}$ does not follow from the abscissa alone; it is a consequence of the counting theorem of §5 (whose proof uses only §§2–4). (ii) $K_{\varphi,k}\to\infty$ almost surely at $a\in I$. (iii) On $|t|B_A(a)=1$, $a=\operatorname{Re}s\in I$, the series $\sum_wt^{|w|}E_\varphi^{-s}K_\varphi^{-u}$ converges absolutely iff $\operatorname{Re}(s+u)>0$. (iv) $\mathcal G_\varphi(\sigma_A,1)<\infty$.

## 5. Counting functions (ANS2-T03)
**Theorem.** Under (D), with $\sigma=\sigma_A$, $\beta=1/\sigma$, and $Z(X)$ equal to $A_\varphi(X)$, to the number $N_x(X)$ of histories from a fixed state $x=a_\varphi n$ with endpoint at most $X$, or to the number $C_\varphi(X)=\sum_{m\le X}A_\varphi(X/m)$ from all sources,
$$\frac{c(\log X)^{\beta}}{\log\log X}\le-\log\frac{Z(X)}{X^{\sigma}}\le C(\log X)^{\beta}.$$

*Proof of the left inequality.* At $a=\sigma$,
$$\frac{A_\varphi(X)}{X^{\sigma}}=\mathbb E\sum_{k\ge0}(a_\varphi K_{\varphi,k})^{-\sigma}\Big(\frac{E_k}{X}\Big)^{\sigma}\mathbf 1_{E_k\le X}.$$
As $E_{k+1}\ge\lambda E_k$, the factors $(E_k/X)^\sigma\mathbf 1$ have bounded sum, so the terms $k\ge k_0$ contribute $O(\mathbb EK_{\varphi,k_0}^{-\sigma})$. For $k<k_0$ take $a\in(1,\sigma)$: at most $X^aB_A(a)^k$ words, since $E_\varphi\ge Q$. Choose $k_0=\lfloor(\sigma-a)\log X/(2\log B_A(a))\rfloor$ and apply §4. Then $N_x\le A_\varphi$, since a history from $x=a_\varphi n$ with endpoint at most $X$ has $E_\varphi(w)\le X$. In $C_\varphi$ use this bound for $m\le\sqrt X$, and $A_\varphi(Y)=O(Y^\sigma)$ for the other $m$, whose total is $O(X^{\sigma-(\sigma-1)/2})$.

*Proof of the right inequality* (first passage). It suffices to treat $x=a_\varphi$: a word admissible from $a_\varphi$ is admissible from $a_\varphi n$ and its endpoint is multiplied by $n$, so $$N_{a_\varphi n}(X)\ge N_{a_\varphi}(X/n),$$ and $N_{a_\varphi}\le A_\varphi\le C_\varphi$. Let $M=\lceil(\log X)^\beta\rceil$ and draw letters with law $\nu_\sigma$ restricted to $N\le M$; let $B_M<1$ be the restricted value of $B_A(\sigma)$. Only primes in $P_0$ or at most $M$ divide $K_\varphi$, so by §3 and monotone convergence $\mathbb E\log K_{\varphi,\infty}\le C_1M$. Thus $\Pr(\log K_{\varphi,\infty}\le4C_1M)\ge3/4$.

Let $L_k=\log Q_k$, $Y=\log X-C_2M$ ($C_2$ large, fixed below), and let $\tau$ be the first $k$ with $L_k>Y-H$. The increments $\xi=\log\mu$ satisfy $\xi\ge\log\lambda$ and have tails bounded uniformly in $M$ by an integrable function. Any unit interval contains at most $1+1/\log\lambda$ of the $L_k$, so
$$\Pr(L_\tau>Y)\le\Big(1+\frac1{\log\lambda}\Big)\sum_{h\ge0}\Pr(\xi>H+h)\le\frac14$$
for a constant $H$. With probability at least $1/2$ both events hold, and $\tau\le\bar k=Y/\log\lambda+1$. The first-passage words $v$ form a prefix-free set, with $\Pr(v)=Q(v)^{-\sigma}B_M^{-|v|}$. Pigeonholing over the at most $e^{4C_1M}$ possible values of $K_\varphi(v)$ gives one $K^\ast$ with
$$\Pr\big(K_\varphi(v)=K^\ast,\ L_\tau\le Y\big)\ge\tfrac12e^{-4C_1M},$$
and the number of such words is at least $\tfrac12e^{-4C_1M}e^{\sigma(Y-H)}B_M^{\bar k}$.

Let $w^\ast$ be the preparation word of ANS-I v003 Lemma 2 for $T=K^\ast$. It uses nesting letters, whose admissibility from $a_\varphi n$ depends only on $n$, so it is admissible from $a_\varphi$, ends at $a_\varphi Q(w^\ast)$ with $K^\ast\mid Q(w^\ast)$, and $\log Q(w^\ast)\le2\log K^\ast+2\theta(M)$ (for $M\ge\max P_0$). Hence every $w^\ast v$ is admissible, and its endpoint is at most $X$ once $C_2M\ge\log a_\varphi+8C_1M+2\theta(M)$. Finally $-\log B_M=O(M^{1-\sigma})$ and $\bar kM^{1-\sigma}=O((\log X)^\beta)$. $\square$

*Remark.* The proof of ANS-I v003 §3 used a central-limit window of width $\sqrt{\log X}$, which is small enough only when $\sigma<2$. Several extensions have $\sigma_A>2$. The first-passage step removes that restriction (and also reproves the original case).

## 6. Derivatives (ANS2-T04)
**Theorem.** Under (D), $D_j=\sum_w(\log E_\varphi)^jE_\varphi^{-\sigma_A}$ satisfies $c^jj^{\sigma_Aj}\le D_j\le(Cj\log j)^{\sigma_Aj}$. Consequently $\mathcal G_\varphi(\cdot,1)$ and $\mathcal Z_\varphi(\cdot,1)$ extend infinitely differentiably to $\operatorname{Re}s\ge\sigma_A$; $\sigma_A$ is a singular point at which neither has a meromorphic continuation; and the infimal Gevrey order of $\mathcal G_\varphi(\cdot,1)$ on the closed half-plane is $\sigma_A$.

*Proof.* The bounds follow from §5 exactly as in ANS-I v003 §4 (here $E_\varphi\ge1$ and $A_\varphi(\sqrt X)=O(X^{\sigma/2})$). Absolute and uniform convergence of all derivatives gives smoothness, also after multiplication by $\zeta$. Landau's theorem gives the singularity; a meromorphic continuation bounded near $\sigma_A$ from the right would be analytic there. $\square$

**First introductions (ANS2-T07).** A prime *occurs* in a nesting letter $N$ if it divides $N(N+1)$; it has been *encountered before step $i$* if it occurs in some nesting letter at a step $j<i$, whether through $N_j$ or through $N_j+1$. Adjoined letters are ignored by this observable. Let $\mathcal I_k$ be the event that, for every $i\le k$ at which the letter is a nesting letter $N_i$, every prime dividing $N_i$ has been encountered before step $i$.

**Theorem.** For every $a>1$, $\Pr(\mathcal I_k)=\exp(-\Theta(k^\beta/\log k))$.

*Proof.* Both halves of ANS-I v003 §5 apply: the letters $\alpha_j$ are always allowed and introduce nothing; for the upper bound consider only $q\notin P_0$.

## 7. Negative drift (ANS2-T05)
**Theorem.** Suppose $\mu_p(\sigma_A)<0$ for some $p\in P_0$. Then the abscissa $\sigma^\ast$ of $\mathcal G_\varphi(\cdot,1)$ satisfies $\sigma_c\le\sigma^\ast<\sigma_A$, where $\sigma_c$ is the nesting exponent. Moreover $\sigma^\ast\le a$ whenever $\Phi_p(a,\theta)<1$ for some $\theta\in[0,1]$, where
$$\Phi_p(a,\theta)=\sum_\ell\mu(\ell)^{-a}p^{\theta aX_p(\ell)}.$$

*Proof.* By Lemma 1, $v_p(K_{\varphi,k})\ge\max(0,-\varphi_p-S_p(k))\ge-\theta(\varphi_p+S_p(k))$. Hence $K_{\varphi,k}^{-a}\le p^{\theta a(\varphi_p+S_p(k))}$ and, by the representation, $\mathcal G_{\varphi,k}(a)\le C\,\Phi_p(a,\theta)^k$. At $a=\sigma_A$ one has $\Phi_p=1$ at $\theta=0$, with $\theta$-derivative $\sigma_A\mu_p(\sigma_A)\log p<0$. So $\Phi_p<1$ for some small $\theta>0$, and by continuity also for some $a<\sigma_A$. The lower bound holds because the nesting words alone have abscissa $\sigma_c$ in every class (§1). $\square$

**Example (computed).** $\alpha=2/\sqrt3$ (torsion order 2, $\alpha>1$): $\sigma_A\approx2.1006$, drift at $3\approx-0.247$, and $\sigma^\ast\le2.062$. **So an expansive torsion multiplier does not in general obey $B_A(\sigma^\ast)=1$.** The exact value of $\sigma^\ast$ is open.

## 8. Coupling between classes (ANS2-T06)
**Proposition.** Let all $w_{jp}\ge0$ for $\alpha=\alpha_j$, applied in class $\varphi$, with image class $\psi$ and integer carry $c$ ($\alpha a_\varphi=a_\psi c$). Then, with $K'=K_\psi(w)$ and $g=\gcd(K',c)$,
$$K_\varphi(\alpha w)=K'/g,\qquad E_\varphi(\alpha w)=E_\psi(w)\cdot c/g .$$

*Proof.* The first step is always admissible and gives $a_\psi cn$; the rest is admissible iff $K'\mid cn$. $\square$

The factor $c/g$ depends on the history $w$. This identity therefore does not express $\mathcal G_\varphi$ through the other $\mathcal G_\psi$ with coefficients independent of $w$: a transfer operator built on first letters has to retain source divisibility.

**Last letter.** For $\alpha$ with non-negative exponents, appending $\alpha$ imposes no new divisibility condition, so $K_\varphi(w\alpha)=K_\varphi(w)$ and $E_\varphi(w\alpha)=\alpha E_\varphi(w)$. Sorted by terminal class, the torsion letters therefore act by an exact linear shift with coefficient $\alpha^{-s}$. An appended nesting letter keeps the class but changes $E$ by $r_N\,N/\gcd(n,N)$, where $n=E_\varphi(w)/a_\psi$ is the integer part of the endpoint in the terminal class $\psi$. This is the part that does not reduce to the class series alone.

## 9. The family of exponents (ANS2-T08)
(i) Under (D), $\sigma_A$ is determined by the real values of the multipliers, and adjoining a multiplier increases the root of $B_A=1$. (ii) The torsion order does not determine it: $\sqrt2$ and $2^{3/2}$ both have order 2 and different roots. (iii) For $\alpha=2^{1/d}$,
$$\sigma_d=\log_4d-\log_4\log d+O(1),\qquad\beta_d=1/\sigma_d\to0 .$$

*Proof of (iii).* $B(\sigma_d)=1-2^{-\sigma_d/d}$, where $B$ is the nesting part. A bounded subsequence of $\sigma_d$ would make the right side tend to $0$ while the left stays away from $0$; so $\sigma_d\to\infty$. Then $B(\sigma_d)=4^{-\sigma_d}(1+o(1))\to0$, hence $\sigma_d/d\to0$ and $4^{-\sigma_d}(1+o(1))=\sigma_dd^{-1}\log2$. Taking logarithms twice gives the formula. $\square$

The constants in §§4–6 depend on $A$; no uniformity in $d$ is claimed.
